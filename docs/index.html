Basel III IRB vs Standardised Capital RWA Comparison
Dashboard Explainer Guide
WHAT THIS SHOWS: Compares bank regulatory capital required under the Basel III Standardised approach against the Advanced Internal Ratings-Based (IRB) supervisory formula across rating grades.
WHY IT MATTERS: Basel III requires banks to hold minimum 8% capital against Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA). Exposure at Default (EAD) represents the total money owed by borrowers at the moment of default ($1.827B total portfolio). Because actual Loss Given Default (LGD) is severe (mean 93.4%), Advanced IRB assigns a 125.6% risk weight ($183.6M capital) vs flat 75.0% Standardised risk weight ($109.6M capital), penalizing uncollateralized risk.
HOW TO READ IT: Look at Risk Weight % across grades A to G. Lower risk grades (Grade A = 94.2% RW) require less capital than higher risk grades (Grade G = 145.7% RW). High risk weights above 100% indicate high default severity requiring capital surcharges.

IFRS 9 vs US CECL Lifetime Provisioning Comparison
Dashboard Explainer Guide
WHAT THIS SHOWS: Compares total accounting credit loss provisions under international IFRS 9 Financial Instruments, US GAAP Current Expected Credit Losses (CECL / FASB ASC 326), and regulatory 12-month Expected Loss (EL).
WHY IT MATTERS: IFRS 9 uses a 3-Stage model reserving 12-month Expected Credit Loss (ECL) for Stage 1 loans and Lifetime ECL only after a Significant Increase in Credit Risk (SICR). US CECL mandates Day-1 Lifetime ECL across ALL performing loans from origination, driving a +$48.99M (+17.6%) higher provision burden.
HOW TO READ IT: Compare Coverage % (Total Provision / EAD). Regulatory 12m EL requires 3.21% coverage ($58.67M), IFRS 9 requires 15.25% coverage ($278.48M), and US CECL requires 17.93% coverage ($327.47M). Higher coverage indicates stricter lifetime loss accounting.

IFRS 9 Portfolio Staging Breakdown
Dashboard Explainer Guide
WHAT THIS SHOWS: Classifies total portfolio Exposure at Default (EAD) and loan counts into IFRS 9 Stage 1 (Performing), Stage 2 (Significant Increase in Credit Risk [SICR]), and Stage 3 (Impaired / Defaulted).
WHY IT MATTERS: Stage 1 loans require 12-month Expected Credit Loss (ECL) provisions, while Stage 2 and Stage 3 require Lifetime ECL reserves. SICR is triggered when current PD increases by $\ge 2.0 imes$ over origination PD or when payments are 30+ Days Past Due (DPD).
HOW TO READ IT: Stage 1 accounts for 80.48% of loans ($1.417B EAD, $30.27M ECL, 2.14% coverage). Stage 2 represents 11.27% of loans ($169.37M EAD, $24.40M ECL, 14.41% coverage). Stage 3 contains 8.25% defaulted loans ($240.00M EAD, $223.80M ECL, 93.25% coverage).
